v2.55 · Night Shift · The Real-Size Earth

The real Earth at 1:1 — crisp, vivid, Capital Rift to the horizon.

New in v2.55: NIGHT SHIFT — your hired hands keep working while the game is closed: every cart with vendors serves the night crowd minute by minute and every crewed mine digs on, the night buyer paying par for every tonne with wages still coming due first (a crew that can’t be paid walks off; a claim that can’t pay for itself at par gets the night off, so the offline net can never go negative). Join back and a While You Were Away popup greets you with exactly what they made — vendors, crews, wages, one NET figure at the bottom — and the shift caps at 24 hours away.

New in v2.54: CREW CALL — clicking a food cart to manage it works again: a single line of code lost in an old rebuild (the recruit-stars line that rates each hire 1–5 stars) had been throwing an error the instant a cart’s business panel tried to draw, so every click on every cart you own — your coffee shop included — silently did nothing, and had since v2.18. The line is restored, so the panel is back: click any cart you own for the live P&L, the menu, hiring your five star-rated vendors and scrapping the cart. New in v2.53: THE WORKS — machines that refine your resources into other resources: twelve refining machines, from a $22,000 Briquette Press (560 t lignite/day → 400 t briquettes) to a $64,000 Alloy Works (320 t nickel/day → 80 t superalloy), each bought outright from the WORKS row on the MINE panel or the THE WORKS button on the exchange board — every game day each machine draws ore from your stockpile and pours refined goods back into it (3 t iron → 1 t steel, 5 t copper → 1 t cathode, 1.6 t quartz → 1 t glass), power billed at $0.25 a tonne processed, and the refined goods trade on the ORE exchange beside the ores (24 rows, the same daily price drift) for more than the ore that went in; any machine scraps back for 60%. New in v2.52: THE ORE EXCHANGE — mining now digs up real resources, and sells them on a market: the ore your crews haul out no longer sells itself — it stacks up tonne by tonne as resources (Iron, Copper, Coal, Bauxite, Nickel, Lignite, Gypsum, Salt, Zinc, Tin, Silver, Quartz) in a stockpile you watch on the MINE panel, and the new ORE button in the bottom bar opens the exchange — twelve prices that move every game day around each ore’s par value (up to ±8% a day, clamped ×0.55–×1.70; watch the ▲▼ day change), SELL HALF / SELL ALL per resource or SELL EVERYTHING in one tap, with wages still due daily in cash and the stockpile counting in your WORTH at today’s prices. New in v2.51: THE MONEY LIST — a leaderboard of the richest, and you’re on it: press the new RANK button in the bottom bar for the board — fifteen rival tycoons around the real Earth (from June Park of Seoul and her first food cart at $26K up to Vittorio “The Duke” Marchetti’s $148.2M old shipping money in Monaco) ranked with you by WORTH, the same cash-plus-everything-you-own figure the ticker shows. Every fortune moves on the 90-second game-day beat — small piles sprint, old money compounds slow, and a daily wobble shuffles the order — while passing a rival (or being passed) toasts it on the spot; your live rank rides the WORTH ticker chip as #N of 16, the board calls out the next name to beat and the dollars between you, and the Empire panel carries a VIEW LIST section. A fresh $8,000 start is #16 of 16 — the climb is the game. New in v2.50: HIRED HANDS — mining is no longer instant: machinery alone digs nothing now, so hire the crew that runs it. Every machine needs its operators (a Wheel Loader takes 1, a Track Excavator 2, a Haul Truck 4, a Rotary Drill 6, a Bucket-Wheel Excavator 12), a short crew runs the ring proportionally slower, and every worker earns $95 a day paid out of the ore they sell — if a payday ever comes up short, the crews walk off on the spot (re-hire when you can pay). Hire and lay off from the machinery yard or the mine’s MANAGE modal, watch up to ten hi-vis workers stand the pit rim while the dig is on, and the mine still lists in your Empire panel with one-tap TELEPORT. New in v2.49: QUICK START — the What’s-New card you see at first login is now three short lines instead of a wall of text. New in v2.48: MOTHERLODE — select an area and mine it out with machinery: press the new MINE button, STAKE A CLAIM, and click two corners of any rectangle of open country (a live gold rectangle follows your cursor between the clicks) — the assay prices what’s underneath, you pay $4/m² for the claim, and then the yard sells you real heavy machinery, from an $18,000 Wheel Loader (60 t/day) to a $520,000 Bucket-Wheel Excavator (4,200 t/day), each one set down wherever you click on the claim’s rim bench (a ghost follows your cursor — green where it fits, red where it doesn’t). The ore sells itself daily at the grade’s spot price, the reserve runs down ton by ton, and the pit you carve cuts a terrace per sixth mined — the dump rim rises past 17 m as the benches step down to the ore face, spoil heaps growing on the corners — until the assay is WORKED OUT and the income stops (sell the machinery back for 60%, or reclaim the land for free). Mines list in the Empire panel with one-tap TELEPORT and count in your WORTH; it all saves with the game. New in v2.47: LANDLORD — rent anything you own back to other people at a price YOU set: an empty street plot, an office suite in a tower, any room or floor you hold, or a farm parcel can go on the rental market at your own daily rate (the MARKET hint shows what tenants pay around here, and the OUTLOOK line calls the demand before you commit). Listing puts it on the market — its own tenant moves out — and prospective tenants view it on the game-day beat: price it near market and they sign almost surely for a 4–12 day lease paying you daily; push past 3× market and nobody ever signs, leaving the unit vacant at $0/day. RENT IT OUT buttons live in the park-a-cart panel, your office panel, the title-deed MANAGE modal and your farmland panel, and the Empire panel tracks your whole rental portfolio — it all saves with the game. New in v2.46: PREMIUM LAND — the buyable farm plots around the world now cost what land should: about ten times more per field (a field that ran $80–$340 now runs roughly $750–$3,400 — a fresh player's $8,000 used to buy fifty fields, now it buys five), while the tenant rent rose about three times to match, so land still pays for itself in about 47 days, the same beat as the machines. Fields you already own keep the price you paid. New in v2.45: MACHINES — fill the rooms and floors you own with money-making machines: pick a floor or room you hold in the building inspector, hit INSTALL MACHINES, and a ghost box follows your cursor on your floor (green where it fits, red where it doesn\’t) — click a spot to set each machine down, from a $2,400 Vending Machine to a $52,000 Server Rack. Every machine pays you every day (a blinking work light says it\’s running), they show in the dollhouse whenever you inspect the building, they list in your Empire panel, they count toward your net worth, and any machine sells back for 60%. It all saves with the game. New in v2.44: PATCHWORK — the open country around every city is now a patchwork of buyable farm parcels, like a satellite view of farm country: rectangles of all shapes and sizes in every shade of green, divided by hedge lines, streaming over every mile of dry land past the suburbs. Zoom out past ~300 m and the whole quilt becomes a land catalog — hover any field for its size and price, click it to buy (or walk up to a FOR SALE signpost at street level). An owned field pays tenant rent every day, its hedge border turns gold, it counts toward your net worth, lists in the Empire panel with one-tap teleport, and sells back any time for 60% — and it all saves with the game. New in v2.43: TITLE DEEDS — every room and floor in the houses and buildings is now yours to buy or rent: click a building, pick a floor (big buildings) or a named room (houses and small shops), and the deal sits right in the panel — buy it outright (yours forever, tenants pay you double rent daily, it counts toward your net worth, sell back any time for 60%) or rent it (a two-day deposit, it pays its rent every day, end the lease whenever you like). Every deed lists in your Empire panel with one-tap travel to its doorstep, your holdings glow gold, and it all saves with the game. New in v2.42: NEIGHBORHOODS — houses and neighborhoods now ring every city: six curved ring roads and sixteen tree-named lanes of houses (lawns, driveways, porches, pitched roofs with dormers and chimneys) fill the green belt from the town edge out to ~2.4 km, dry land only, and every house is a real building — click it and the inspector opens it, rooms and all. The city and its neighborhoods stay standing while you walk them, and the woodland belt picks up where the last yard ends. New in v2.41: FLOOR PLAN — clicking any building now opens it with an inspector panel: big buildings list every floor (pick one and the storeys above lift away, leaving that floor's furnished rooms open to the sky, with the camera gliding level with your pick), while houses and small buildings list their rooms by name — Living Room, Kitchen, Bedroom, Stock Room… — and the room you pick glows on the floor. Leasable towers carry their LEASE SUITE button in the same panel, and ✕ or ESC closes it. New in v2.40: OPEN HOUSE — walk up to any building (on foot, or pan the camera right up to one) and it opens like a dollhouse: the shell slices open at waist height and every floor shows its furnished rooms — floor slabs with stair cores, partitions with doorways, offices, apartments, lobbies and shops. New in v2.39: WORLD FOREST — trees now grow all around the world besides in the cities: beyond every city's woodland belt, the open country carries real 3D forest (clumped stands blending into open savanna), placed with the same dry-land gate as everything else — never in water, never on roads, never on vendor plots. The whole world is the real Earth at TRUE SCALE — 111,320 meters per degree, 40,007 km around the equator — and it now reads the way capitalrift.com actually looks: no haze at any zoom, vivid high-contrast mottled olive grass, teal seas and true teal great lakes meeting an azure sky at a crisp horizon, and distant cities standing as dark masses on the land. New in v2.38: DEEP-BLUE LAKES — the land shield no longer renders under lake water at all: lake-touching land cells build from the lake's own raster on a 6.9 km to 215 m grid, so the water is the only surface there and nothing can bleed green through it at any altitude (this was the last green-in-the-lake mechanism — the giant land triangles carried meters of depth error at far zoom, past any separation the water could ride at; verified pixel-exact from street level to the 23,000 km view). New in v2.37: WHOLE-WORLD ZOOM — the zoom ceiling lifts from 34 km to 23,000 km, so you can zoom from street level all the way out until the entire real Earth (40,075 km wide) fits in one view: green continents, blue oceans and the true-shape great lakes, with city beacons visible from orbit and every flat ground layer drawing in explicit height order so nothing flickers at the new extreme zooms. v2.36: TRUE LAKES — every great lake now uses real 10-meter-resolution outlines (Lake Michigan in its actual shape, Green Bay and the Door Peninsula included), the water renders solid clean blue shore to shore with zero holes (an indexed-geometry bug had silently dropped two-thirds of every lake fragment since the Earth rewrite — the "green in the lake" the whole time), and the camera is unchained: the distance cap between your view and your tycoon is gone, so pan anywhere on Earth with detail streaming wherever you look. v2.35: green pastures and blue lakes — the land is fresh mottled pasture green, every sea and lake is clean open blue, and a woodland belt of trees rings every city in the pastures. v2.34: the great lakes finally render as solid water — a clipping bug had dropped 26% of Lake Michigan's surface and grass showed right through it — and the last fog gradient is gone too: the air stays clear all the way to the edge of the world view at every zoom. v2.33: the continental interior finally RENDERS (the old land builder only drew cells the coastline passed through — whole states were holes) with a concavity patch for bays and estuaries, NaN-guarded world tiles. v2.30: depth-stable ground at true scale (no z-fight stripes under cities, inter-city roads no longer swallowed); v2.29: inter-city roads END at the first street of the city they serve — a real T-junction; v2.27: true scale — distant skylines stand as real metro sprawl on true coastlines, coastal roads become causeways with guard pylons, and older saves migrate. Every city still stands in the world before you get there (v2.26), instant travel stays (search, world map [M], orbit flight), and nothing generates in water.
